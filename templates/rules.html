What is a Keynesian Beauty Contest?

In The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money (1936), economist John Maynard Keynes described a newspaper competition where readers picked the six prettiest faces from a hundred photographs. The prize was awarded to whoever chose the faces that were most popular overall.

"It is not a case of choosing those faces that, to the best of one's judgment, are really the prettiest, nor even those that average opinion genuinely thinks the prettiest. We have reached the third degree where we devote our intelligences to anticipating what average opinion expects the average opinion to be."

How This Elimination Game Works

  • Integer Inputs: In each round, every player chooses an integer from 0 to 100.
  • The Target: All guesses are averaged ($\mu$), and multiplied by the target fraction (usually $\frac{2}{3}$): $$\text{Target} = \frac{2}{3} \times \text{Average}$$
  • Winner(s): The player whose guess is closest to the Target wins the round. Ties are permitted; tied winners both win.
  • Lives System: Every participant begins with 10 lives (or as configured by the admin). Winners lose 0 lives. All non-winners lose 1 life.
  • Elimination: When a player reaches 0 lives, they are eliminated and spectate the remaining rounds. The last survivor wins!

Special Optional Rules

The admin can activate or deactivate these special game theory rules between rounds:

1. Duplicate Clash Penalty

If two or more players submit the exact same number, their guesses clash! They are immediately disqualified from winning that round and each lose 1 life.

2. Exact Mean Penalty (-2 Lives)

If any player guesses exactly the rounded mean of all submitted numbers, the stakes double! All non-winning players lose 2 lives instead of 1.

3. Final Duel: 0 vs 100

When only 2 players remain alive, if one player selects 0 and the other selects 100, the player who picked 100 wins the round!
